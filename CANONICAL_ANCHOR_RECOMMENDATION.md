# Canonical Anchor Recommendation: Annambhaṭṭa's *Tarka-Saṃgraha*, with Mithila-Institute Consultation as the Primary-Source Supplement

**Date:** 2026-05-09
**Resolves:** SAN-OQ-001 ("Mithila vs Bengal canonical anchor"), SCH-OQ-001 ("Whose Navya-Nyāya?")
**Author:** Advisor on classical Indian philosophy
**Audience:** Founder, pramana-eval

---

## 1. The recommendation

**Anchor the schema in Annambhaṭṭa's *Tarka-Saṃgraha* (TS) plus its auto-commentary *Tarka-Dīpikā*, in the Athalye-Bodas 1930 critical edition, with Mithila-Institute consultation as the primary-source supplement and Phillips-Tatacharya 2004/Phillips 2012 as the Gaṅgeśa-tier escalation when a phase requires it.** This is the Tarka-Saṃgraha digest line, NOT the Bengal line, and NOT pure Mithila Gaṅgeśa-line.

---

## 2. Why this and not the other two

**Reason 1 — A non-Sanskrit-reading founder cannot defend the Bengal or pure-Mithila line.** Raghunātha Śiromaṇi's *Padārtha-tattva-nirūpaṇa* exists in English only through Karl Potter's 1957 Harvard-Yenching translation and his EIP Vol. 6 summaries; Raghunātha's *Dīdhiti* commentary on TC is *untranslated*. The Bengal line is the most technically demanding stratum of Navya-Nyāya — Mathurānātha, Jagadīśa, Gadādhara — and is essentially unreadable without a *paṇḍita* at one's elbow. A founder with no formal Sanskrit must not anchor a schema there. The pure Mithila line (Gaṅgeśa's TC itself) has an English translation only of the Pratyakṣa-khaṇḍa and parts of the Anumāna-khaṇḍa (Phillips & N. S. Ramanuja Tatacharya, *Epistemology of Perception*, AIBS/Columbia 2004; Phillips, *Epistemology of Perception in Classical India*, MLBD 2020 expansion; Phillips 2012 Routledge volume for inference). The Sanskrit text is in Bibliotheca Indica (Kāmākhyānātha Tarkavāgīśa, ASB Calcutta 1884–1901) and Chowkhamba — not freely or fully translated. Citing TC granularly is precisely the failure that produced the v0.1 audit findings 1.1–1.3.

**Reason 2 — Tarka-Saṃgraha is operationally evaluable in the way the schema needs.** TS is ~15 pages of root text with the *Dīpikā* attached, organized by *uddeśa* (enumeration) → *lakṣaṇa* (definition) → *parīkṣā* (examination). Annambhaṭṭa already gives the schema what it needs in canonical form: the four *pramāṇa*s with sub-types (Phase 6), the *pañcāvayava* (Phase 11), *vyāpti* in a teachable extensional form (Phase 8), the Navya five-fold *hetvābhāsa* — *savyabhicāra, viruddha, satpratipakṣa, asiddha* with sub-types, *bādhita* — (Phase 13), and *abhāva* as a categorial *padārtha* (Phase 14). Athalye-Bodas paragraph numbering is *the* citation standard scholars actually use, is named explicitly in CITATIONS.md (per SCH-A-002), and the entire edition is on archive.org. This is the only choice on which a reproducibility-hawk reviewer (R5) can verify a citation in 15 minutes without library access.

**Reason 3 — TS is school-neutral on the Mithila/Bengal split, which is what the project actually wants.** Annambhaṭṭa (17th c., Maharashtrian-Tamil) wrote AFTER both Mithila Gaṅgeśa and Bengal Raghunātha, synthesizing both into the standard pedagogical canon that every Sanskrit student in India still learns first. Anchoring there does not commit the project to a sectarian intra-tradition position the founder is in no position to defend. It is also exactly what a Mithila Institute *paṇḍita* would expect a non-specialist to anchor on; arriving with a "we anchor in TS" letter signals respectful self-positioning, while arriving with "we anchor in Gaṅgeśa's *Anumāna-khaṇḍa*" signals overreach. The Mithila Institute relationship is *strengthened*, not weakened, by anchoring in TS and citing TC through the Phillips-Tatacharya translation only when a phase (8, 9, 10) genuinely requires Gaṅgeśa's specific innovation.

## 3. What this commits the schema to

**Primary text register (replaces the v0.1 fabricated section numbers):**
- *Tarka-Saṃgraha* + *Tarka-Dīpikā*, Athalye (ed.) & Bodas (tr.), 2nd edition, Bhandarkar Oriental Research Institute, Poona, 1930. Archive.org scan canonical. Cite by Athalye paragraph, named explicitly: "TS, Athalye §N (1930 ed., p. M)."
- *Nyāya-Sūtra* + *Bhāṣya*: Gaṅgānātha Jhā translation (1939, MLBD reprint 1984). Cite by NS sūtra number.
- *Tattvacintāmaṇi*, Anumāna-khaṇḍa: Phillips 2012 (*Epistemology of the Knowledge Sources of the Nyāya School*, Routledge) and Phillips & Tatacharya 2004 (*Epistemology of Perception*, AIBS/Columbia) by translator + page. NEVER cite "TC Anumāna-khaṇḍa 5.1" — that decimal is SEP editorial, the v0.1 error.
- *Ātmatattvaviveka* of Udayana: N. S. Dravid translation (ICPR, 1995) for Phase 14.
- Raghunātha's *Padārtha-tattva-nirūpaṇa*: Potter (Harvard-Yenching, 1957) ONLY where Phase 14's *abhāva*-as-real-category needs the Bengal-line cross-check. Not load-bearing.

**Translator precedence:** Phillips for analytic-extensional readings of *vyāpti*; Mohanty (*Gaṅgeśa's Theory of Truth*, 1989) for cognitive-act readings of *paramarśa*; Matilal (*The Character of Logic in India*, 1998) for the Navya-Nyāya-vs-Buddhist-logic framing; Bhattacharyya & Potter EIP Vol. 6 for the Gaṅgeśa-to-Raghunātha terrain. Disclose each translator's framing per SAN-F-001.

**Phase adjustments:**
- **Phases 8, 9, 10** (Gaṅgeśa-derived: *vyāpti*, *upādhi*, *parāmarśa* + *pakṣadharmatā*) remain Gaṅgeśa-anchored but cite Phillips-Tatacharya 2004 / Phillips 2012 by *page*, with TS paragraph cross-references where Annambhaṭṭa preserves the same definition. Drop every "TC Anumāna-khaṇḍa 5.X" decimal.
- **Phase 13** (*hetvābhāsa*) cleanly anchors in TS with Annambhaṭṭa's Navya five — this is the natural home and removes the v0.1 contradiction between the 6-phase prompt (which used Gautama's old list) and the 15-phase draft (which uses Annambhaṭṭa's). Per SAN-C-004, migrate the 6-phase prompt to Annambhaṭṭa's list to harmonize.
- **Phase 6** (*pramāṇa* selection) and **Phase 11** (*pañcāvayava*) anchor in TS directly.
- **Phases 1–5, 7, 12, 15** anchor in NS + Vātsyāyana's *Bhāṣya* (Gaṅgānātha Jhā translation), since these are Old-Nyāya-grounded categories Annambhaṭṭa preserves without innovation.
- **Phase 14** (*anupalabdhi/abhāva*) anchors in Udayana (Dravid 1995) with TS *abhāva*-as-*padārtha* secondary citation; Raghunātha tertiary.

## 5. The Mithila Institute angle

**The founder can ship without the Mithila Institute's explicit endorsement, but MUST not ship without documented outreach.** Per SAN-E-001, three documented contact attempts over a 4-week response window is the floor. Anchoring in TS rather than TC reduces the dependency on Mithila — TS is school-neutral and Athalye-Bodas was published in Maharashtra, not Mithila — so a non-response from the institute is not fatal. But the outreach must happen.

**What the letter specifically asks:**
1. Whether the institute would assign a *paṇḍita* (named, with consent) to read the schema document and Sanskrit register, against modest honorarium.
2. Whether the institute objects to the framing "anchored in Annambhaṭṭa's *Tarka-Saṃgraha*, supplemented by Gaṅgeśa's *Tattvacintāmaṇi* via Phillips-Tatacharya, with respectful acknowledgement of the Mithila *paramparā* as the originating lineage of Navya-Nyāya proper."
3. Whether the institute would accept named acknowledgement in the NOTICE as the originating institutional home of Gaṅgeśa Upādhyāya's tradition, regardless of whether they engage further.
4. What the institute's preferred attribution language is (their own preferred phrasing should override anything the founder drafts).

**The endorsement-vs-citation distinction:** Choosing TS as anchor rather than TC means the project does NOT need Mithila's endorsement to be philologically defensible — Annambhaṭṭa's authority is Annambhaṭṭa's, not Mithila's. What the project DOES need is respectful, documented engagement with Mithila as the living institutional home of the originating lineage. Three letters and four weeks is the cost. A non-response, documented, satisfies SAN-E-001.

## 6. Risks of this choice

**Risk 1 — A Bengal-line specialist reviewer (R2 archetype) objects that the schema sidelines Raghunātha, Mathurānātha, Jagadīśa, Gadādhara, missing the technical maturity of Navya-Nyāya.**
*Mitigation:* The schema's "Whose Navya-Nyāya?" disclosure (SCH-B-003) names this explicitly: "We anchor in TS as the standard pedagogical synthesis, with TC supplementing for Gaṅgeśa's specific innovations. We do NOT engage the Bengal-line refinements (Raghunātha forward), because the technical apparatus required (Mathurānātha's *Mathurī*, Jagadīśa's *Jāgadīśī*, Gadādhara's *Gādādharī*) is beyond the founder's competence and beyond the schema's scope. Future versions may extend toward the Bengal line conditional on competent collaboration." This is the honest disclosure that pre-empts the attack.

**Risk 2 — A Gaṅgeśa-purist reviewer (an S1-archetype Mithila *paṇḍita*) objects that anchoring in Annambhaṭṭa is "Navya-Nyāya simplified for beginners" and not really Navya-Nyāya proper.**
*Mitigation:* The objection is partly fair and the schema concedes it. The mitigation is exactly the consultation letter: ask the Mithila Institute what an honest non-Sanskritist OSS project should do, follow their advice, name a *paṇḍita* if one is willing. The framing is not "we did Navya-Nyāya" but "we built an LLM benchmark structurally informed by classical Naiyāyika reasoning, anchored in the standard pedagogical text and supplemented for Gaṅgeśa-specific innovations." The schema document's "What this measures" section (SCH-F-001) MUST commit to (a) "structured-output benchmark for cognitive operations Navya-Nyāya names," not (b) "the model has achieved Navya-Nyāya cognition."

**Risk 3 — The Sathish 2026 mirroring critique sharpens, because TS is also where most accessible Navya-Nyāya English summary lives, so Sathish presumably also drew from TS.**
*Mitigation:* This is a manageable risk if the schema is honest about contribution accounting (SCH-C-004): the divergence from Sathish lives in Phases 8, 9, 10 (Gaṅgeśa-specific via Phillips-Tatacharya, NOT TS) and Phase 14 (Udayana via Dravid, NOT TS). The TS-anchored phases (6, 11, 13) ARE shared with Sathish; the schema must say so, demoting the "60% independent" claim to 30–45% as the audit demands. The honesty strengthens, not weakens, defensibility.

Write to the Mithila Institute asking for review and named acknowledgement. Document outreach. Ship after the SCH-G-005 minimum bar (Sanskrit-fluent reviewer + citation auditor + one engaged Indian-philosophy academic) is cleared.
