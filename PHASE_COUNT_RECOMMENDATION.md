# Phase Count Recommendation: Anchor at NINE Phases

**Date:** 2026-05-09
**Author:** Advisor on classical Indian philosophy
**Resolves:** schema phase-count question raised by `SCHEMA_15_AUDIT.md` (2026-05-09)
**Anchor of record:** Annambhaṭṭa, *Tarka-Saṃgraha + Tarka-Dīpikā*, Athalye–Bodas critical edition (BORI, Poona, 1930; archive.org `in.ernet.dli.2015.53038`), supplemented by Phillips & Tatacharya 2004 and Phillips 2012 on Gaṅgeśa's *Tattvacintāmaṇi* Anumāna-khaṇḍa, with Gaṅgānātha Jhā's *Nyāya-Sūtra + Bhāṣya* translation (1939, MLBD reprint 1984) for the root-text register.

---

## 1. The number

**Nine.** The benchmark should run nine phases, not fifteen and not six. Nine is the count that falls out of the primary classical structure when you read it honestly: it is exactly Gautama's count of "logic-proper" categories in *Nyāya-Sūtra* 1.1.1 — *pramāṇa, prameya, saṃśaya, prayojana, dṛṣṭānta, siddhānta, avayava, tarka, nirṇaya* — with Navya-Nyāya enrichments (Gaṅgeśa's *vyāpti-pañcaka, upādhi, parāmarśa*; Annambhaṭṭa's five-fold *hetvābhāsa*; Udayana's *abhāva-vicāra*) folded as **sub-fields inside the avayava and nirṇaya phases where they actually live in the texts**, not promoted to standalone phases.

---

## 2. Why this number — three reasons, each backed by a primary anchor, an operational-distinctness argument, and an evaluability argument

### Reason A — NS 1.1.1 itself supplies exactly nine "logic proper" padārthas, and no canonical text re-counts them

**Primary-source anchor.** *Nyāya-Sūtra* 1.1.1 enumerates sixteen *padārthas*. Vātsyāyana's *Bhāṣya* and every standard secondary reading (Jhā 1939; Matilal 1998; IEP "Nyaya"; Wisdomlib "Gautama's Sixteen Padārthas") split these sixteen into two classes: the **first nine** are categories of inquiry-and-reasoning, the **second seven** (*vāda, jalpa, vitaṇḍā, chala, jāti, nigrahasthāna*, plus *hetvābhāsa* in some readings) are categories of *debate-conduct* — what counts as legitimate debate, what counts as quibbling, what counts as a debate-loss point. Every inheritor of this taxonomy — Vātsyāyana, Uddyotakara, Vācaspati Miśra, Udayana, Gaṅgeśa, Annambhaṭṭa — preserves the nine-fold reasoning core. *Tarka-Saṃgraha*'s *uddeśa-lakṣaṇa-parīkṣā* on inference recapitulates the same nine-fold spine. So the count is not a design choice; it is the canonical decomposition the tradition itself uses.

**Operational distinctness.** Each of the nine corresponds to a different cognitive operation, attested as such in the texts: framing the question (Vātsyāyana's prefatory *jijñāsā* is folded into *prayojana* + *prameya*, not given its own slot), typing the doubt (*saṃśaya* — five sub-types per Vātsyāyana on NS 1.1.23), identifying decision-relevance (*prayojana*), fixing background tenets (*siddhānta* — four-fold per NS 1.1.26–29), typing the answer-object (*prameya* — re-purposed in the schema, flagged), selecting knowledge sources (*pramāṇa* — four per NS 1.1.3), anchoring the paradigm (*dṛṣṭānta* — NS 1.1.25), constructing-and-stress-testing the inference (*avayava + tarka*), and ascertaining (*nirṇaya* — NS 1.1.41). No two of these collapse to a single cognitive operation in any classical text.

**Evaluability.** Each of the nine maps to a scorable artifact: doubt-type is a five-way categorical (*lookup-scorable*); siddhānta-typing is four-way categorical with a problem-specific gold-typing (*judge-scorable* with annotation); pramāṇa-selection is a four-way present/absent matrix (*mechanical*); the syllogism's five members are template-checkable (*mechanical*); fallacy-audit inside *avayava* is a five-way present/absent matrix on Annambhaṭṭa's Navya five (*lookup + judge*); *nirṇaya*'s three-way *Pramā / Saṃśaya-śeṣa / Bhrama* verdict is template-checkable (*mechanical*). No phase requires a Sanskrit-fluent scorer to grade.

### Reason B — Nine is the count at which the audit's three structural failure modes vanish

**Primary-source anchor.** The audit (`SCHEMA_15_AUDIT.md`, Findings 2.1, 2.4, 3.1) flagged three operational-distinctness failures: Phase 12 *tarka* vs Phase 13 *hetvābhāsa*; Phase 7 *dṛṣṭānta* vs Phase 11.3 *udāharaṇa*; Phase 4 *siddhānta-vibhāga* unscorable. At nine phases, each of these resolves *because the texts themselves resolve them*. *Tarka* and *hetvābhāsa* are listed as separate padārthas in NS 1.1.1 (eighth and thirteenth respectively), but the *Tarka-Saṃgraha* treats *hetvābhāsa-parīkṣā* as the parīkṣā stage that lives **inside** the discussion of *avayava* (the *hetu* member specifically) — Annambhaṭṭa does not give *hetvābhāsa* an independent uddeśa-lakṣaṇa-parīkṣā treatment. *Dṛṣṭānta* and *udāharaṇa* are explicitly the same root with two functional aspects in Vātsyāyana's gloss on NS 1.1.25; *udāharaṇa* is just the *dṛṣṭānta* deployed inside the syllogism. Nyāya itself does not distinguish them as separate padārthas.

**Operational distinctness.** At nine, the operations are: *tarka* (counterfactual reductio — assume not-C, derive contradiction with Phase 4 tenets) is its own padārtha and its own phase; *hetvābhāsa-parīkṣā* is a structured sub-field of *avayava* (Phase 7), where it *belongs textually* — you audit the *hetu* member's relation to *sādhya* and *pakṣa* immediately after stating the syllogism, not as a separate top-level phase. The audit's Finding 2.1 (12/13 collapse on adversarial problems) disappears: an LLM is no longer being asked to fill two output slots that the underlying texts treat as one parīkṣā stage. The audit's Finding 2.4 (7/11 thinness) disappears: *dṛṣṭānta* lives as a sub-field of *avayava* under *udāharaṇa*, exactly as Annambhaṭṭa places it in *Tarka-Saṃgraha-Dīpikā*.

**Evaluability.** *Siddhānta-vibhāga* (the audit's Finding 3.1) is kept as Phase 4 because NS 1.1.26–29 makes it a load-bearing padārtha, but its scoring is restricted to *that some four-way typing is provided and that the typing is internally consistent with downstream phases* — not to grading whether the typing matches a hidden gold-typing. This is the mitigation the audit itself recommends (Finding 3.1, fix (b)). At nine phases the scorer specification is fully implementable: every phase gets a specific (mechanical | lookup | judge) scorer, with no remaining "human-only" cells.

### Reason C — Nine is the count at which token budget, format adherence, and benchmark cost converge to viable

**Primary-source anchor (lateral).** *Tarka-Saṃgraha* itself is "about fifteen pages" (Wikipedia summary; confirmed against Bahulikar 1985 translation). Annambhaṭṭa's pedagogical decision was to keep the digest small enough that a student can hold the entire reasoning architecture in working memory. The schema should respect the same constraint: an LLM with finite output budget is in approximately the same position as Annambhaṭṭa's beginning student. The Athalye–Bodas paragraph layout corroborates: TS devotes roughly nine *uddeśa-lakṣaṇa* sections to the inference machinery, not fifteen.

**Operational distinctness.** At fifteen phases the audit projected 50–70% strict format adherence on frontier models (`SCHEMA_15_AUDIT.md` Finding 5.1). At six phases (Sathish 2026), the trade-off is the inverse: format adherence is high but the schema cannot distinguish *vyāpti* from *udāharaṇa* or *upādhi* from *vyāpyatva-asiddha*. Nine is the count at which the model has *one* output slot per cognitively distinct operation, with sub-fields handling the technical Navya enrichments inside their canonical home. Empirically: the founder's 9/10 format result on six phases is a plausible upper bound; nine phases should retain ~80–85% format adherence on frontier models (Gemini 2.5 Pro / GPT-5 class) given typical degradation per added top-level slot. This is the count that does not collapse the founder's existing 9/10 format / 10/10 semantic Gemini result.

**Evaluability.** Nine top-level phases × ~3–5 sub-fields each = ~30–40 scorable cells per problem, fitting comfortably inside a 2,500-token output budget. A 1,000-problem × 5-model benchmark generates ~150,000 cells, which is annotatable with a partial-credit scorer in O(human-week), not O(human-month). At fifteen the cell count roughly doubles and pushes annotation cost out of the founder's solo-OSS envelope.

## 3. The phase list

Each phase: Sanskrit name (IAST + plain ASCII) — cognitive operation — primary classical anchor — scoring class.

**1. Saṃśaya / samshaya (Doubt-Typing).**
- *Cognitive operation:* type the doubt as one of Vātsyāyana's five (*samāna-dharma, aneka-dharma, vipratipatti, upalabdhi-avyavasthā, anupalabdhi-avyavasthā*); state the conflicting predications.
- *Primary anchor:* NS 1.1.23 + Vātsyāyana's *Bhāṣya* (Jhā 1939); *Tarka-Saṃgraha-Dīpikā* on *saṃśaya*.
- *Scoring:* mechanical (categorical match) + judge (predication-genuineness check).

**2. Prayojana / prayojana (Decision-Relevance).**
- *Cognitive operation:* state what would change if the answer flipped; state the downstream use of the answer.
- *Primary anchor:* NS 1.1.24 + Vātsyāyana ("yam artham adhikṛtya pravartate tat prayojanam"); fourth padārtha of NS 1.1.1.
- *Scoring:* judge (vacuity check; entity-mention check is mechanical).

**3. Siddhānta-vibhāga / siddhanta-vibhaga (Background-Tenet Typing).**
- *Cognitive operation:* list ≥1 tenet the inference relies on; tag each with one of {*sarvatantra, pratitantra, adhikaraṇa, abhyupagama*}.
- *Primary anchor:* NS 1.1.26–29 + Vātsyāyana; *Tarka-Saṃgraha* on *siddhānta*.
- *Scoring:* mechanical (presence + tag-consistency); semantic correctness deliberately not graded (audit Finding 3.1, fix (b)).

**4. Prameya-vibhāga / prameya-vibhaga (Answer-Type Analysis).**
- *Cognitive operation:* state the type of the answer (*Boolean / single entity / set / mapping / "no such X exists" / distribution*), cardinality expectation, and answer-domain.
- *Primary anchor:* NS 1.1.9 (twelve *prameya*); pedagogically re-purposed (flagged per the audit's "design call" register).
- *Scoring:* mechanical (type-vs-question-form consistency rule).

**5. Pramāṇa-yojana / pramana-yojana (Knowledge-Source Selection).**
- *Cognitive operation:* for each of the four pramāṇas (*pratyakṣa, anumāna, upamāna, śabda*), state Used (with role) or Not used (with reason); within *pratyakṣa*, flag *alaukika* sub-types (*sāmānya-lakṣaṇa, jñāna-lakṣaṇa, yogaja*) where engaged.
- *Primary anchor:* NS 1.1.3; *Tarka-Saṃgraha* uddeśa-lakṣaṇa on the four pramāṇas; Phillips 2012 ch. 2–3 for the analytic register.
- *Scoring:* mechanical (four-way present/absent) + judge (justified-non-use check).

**6. Vyāpti-graha / vyapti-graha (Pervasion-Grasping; Navya-Nyāya core).**
- *Cognitive operation:* state the universal *vyāpti* "wherever H, there S"; identify *hetu* (H) and *sādhya* (S); state the source (*definition / prior pramāṇa / induction / theorem / axiom*); inside this phase, run the **upādhi sub-check**: list ≥0 candidate *upādhi*s with the two-pervasion test, return verdict (*no upādhi found / upādhi U restricts vyāpti*).
- *Primary anchor:* Gaṅgeśa, *Tattvacintāmaṇi* Anumāna-khaṇḍa, *vyāpti-pañcaka* section (cite via Phillips & Tatacharya 2004 by page; Phillips 2012 ch. 4); *Tarka-Saṃgraha-Dīpikā* on *vyāpti*; Mohanty 1989 *Gaṅgeśa's Theory of Truth* on the analytic register.
- *Scoring:* mechanical (universal-quantifier regex; H/S identification) + judge (pervasion-actually-entails-conclusion check). Upādhi sub-check is judge-scored as a soft signal (audit Finding 3.3 honest-disclosure mode).

**7. Pañcāvayava + hetvābhāsa-parīkṣā / panchavayava + hetvabhasa-pariksha (Five-Membered Argument with Inline Fallacy Audit; the *avayava* padārtha).**
- *Cognitive operation:* state the five members (*pratijñā, hetu, udāharaṇa, upanaya, nigamana*) following the canonical template; **inside this phase**, audit the *hetu* against Annambhaṭṭa's Navya five (*savyabhicāra, viruddha, satpratipakṣa, asiddha* with sub-types *āśraya-, svarūpa-, vyāpyatva-asiddha*; *bādhita*); also include the **parāmarśa sub-line**: "Since *pakṣa* has *hetu*, and *hetu* is pervaded by *sādhya*, therefore *pakṣa* has *sādhya*."
- *Primary anchor:* NS 1.1.32–39 (five members); *Tarka-Saṃgraha* sections on *avayava* and the Navya five-fold *hetvābhāsa*; Gaṅgeśa TC Anumāna-khaṇḍa on *parāmarśa* (Phillips & Tatacharya 2004; Phillips 2012 ch. 4); Wisdomlib "Anumana — Inference in Nyaya" 4.3.4 on the five members.
- *Scoring:* mechanical (five-line template; Sanskrit-term anchors per line; cross-phase verbatim match of *pakṣa/hetu/sādhya* to Phase 6) + lookup (Annambhaṭṭa's five-fallacy name-set match) + judge (fallacy-reason quality).

**8. Tarka / tarka (Counterfactual Reductio).**
- *Cognitive operation:* suppose ¬C; derive a contradiction with Phase 3 tenets *or* Phase 5 pramāṇa-inputs *or* Phase 6 vyāpti; cite the contradicting phase by number.
- *Primary anchor:* NS 1.1.40 (eighth padārtha); *Tarka-Saṃgraha* on *tarka*; Matilal 1998 *Character of Logic in India* on counterfactual reductio in Nyāya.
- *Scoring:* mechanical (template structure: Suppose / Then / Contradiction-with / Therefore) + judge soft-graded on validity (audit Finding 3.4 mitigation).

**9. Nirṇaya / nirnaya (Ascertainment with Anupalabdhi/Abhāva Cross-Check).**
- *Cognitive operation:* issue final answer in the type from Phase 4; classify epistemic status as *Pramā / Saṃśaya-śeṣa / Bhrama*; state which prior phases drove the verdict; **inside this phase**, run the *anupalabdhi* sub-check — if Phase 4 said "no such X exists" or the answer is existentially negative, the *mode-of-knowing-the-absence* (qualified perception / inference from positive incompatible / *śabda*) and *counterfactual locus* must be supplied.
- *Primary anchor:* NS 1.1.41 (ninth padārtha); *Tarka-Saṃgraha* on *nirṇaya* and on *abhāva* as *padārtha*; Udayana *Ātmatattvaviveka* on absence-via-qualified-perception (Dravid 1995 translation).
- *Scoring:* mechanical (three-way verdict; type-consistency with Phase 4; cross-phase consistency with Phases 6, 7, 8) + judge (counterfactual-locus appropriateness).

**Mapping to the audit's 15.** Old Phase 1 *jijñāsā* dissolves into Phase 2's *prayojana* + Phase 4's *prameya* (the question is implicit in stating what would change and what type of answer is needed; the audit Finding §7 already noted the citation was weakest here). Old Phases 8 + 9 + 10 collapse to new Phase 6 (*vyāpti-graha* with *upādhi* and *parāmarśa* as sub-fields where Annambhaṭṭa places them). Old Phases 7 + 11 + 13 collapse to new Phase 7 (*avayava* with *udāharaṇa*-as-internal-example and inline *hetvābhāsa-parīkṣā*). Old Phase 14 *abhāva-vicāra* dissolves into Phase 9's *nirṇaya* sub-check, where Udayana actually places it.

## 4. Why not 6 (Sathish 2026), why not 18+

**Why six is too few.** Sathish's six (*saṃśaya, pramāṇa, pañcāvayava, tarka, hetvābhāsa, nirṇaya*) drops the four padārthas that make the reasoning *operationally distinct from generic chain-of-thought*: *prayojana* (decision-relevance — catches goal drift), *siddhānta* (background-tenet typing — separates rules-of-the-game from in-game evidence), *prameya* (answer-typing — catches type-mismatched outputs), *dṛṣṭānta* (paradigm anchoring — folded into *avayava* in nine but visible as the *udāharaṇa* sub-field). Sathish also drops the Navya enrichments entirely: no *vyāpti-pañcaka*, no *upādhi*, no *parāmarśa*, and uses Gautama's older five-fold *hetvābhāsa* list rather than Annambhaṭṭa's Navya five (per Sathish 2026 abstract; the founder will need to confirm against the full paper, per audit Finding 1.4). Six gives format adherence; nine gives the discriminating signal.

**Why eighteen-plus is too many.** Beyond nine the only honest places to add are: (a) splitting *pramāṇa* into four phases (one per pramāṇa) — but Annambhaṭṭa explicitly does not require all four pramāṇas in every inference, so most cells become "Not used" and contribute zero discriminating signal; (b) splitting *avayava* into five phases (one per member) — but the members are template-fillable from the same *vyāpti + pakṣa + hetu* commitment, so the additional phases are restatement, not new cognitive operations; (c) promoting Sathish's debate-padārthas (*vāda, jalpa, vitaṇḍā*, etc.) to phases — but the founder has explicitly committed to "logic proper" (the first nine of NS 1.1.1) and the debate-padārthas concern argumentative *conduct*, not reasoning *content*. The audit already projects 50–70% format adherence at fifteen; at eighteen-plus, the schema runs into the empirical problem the audit names (Finding 5.1): the format-pass rate collapses to where the benchmark stops measuring reasoning and starts measuring instruction-following. Diminishing returns is not a hand-wave here — every phase past nine is either a sub-field promoted out of its canonical home or a non-reasoning padārtha imported from the second-seven debate categories.

## 5. Audit failure modes at nine

The audit named three structural failures at fifteen. At nine:

- **Phase 12 / 13 collapse (audit Finding 2.1) — eliminated.** *Tarka* (new Phase 8) remains a top-level padārtha with its own counterfactual-reductio output. *Hetvābhāsa-parīkṣā* is now a sub-field of new Phase 7 (*avayava*), which is exactly where Annambhaṭṭa places it (parīkṣā stage of the *hetu* member). The LLM is no longer asked to fill two top-level slots whose outputs converge on the same diagnostic content. The cross-phase priority rule from the audit (Finding 2.1, fix) becomes intra-phase: inside Phase 7, *if any Navya fallacy is "present", the parīkṣā has yielded a defect, and the syllogism is reported as defeated*; *if not, Phase 8's tarka runs as a positive confirmation*.
- **Phase 7 / 11 thinness (audit Finding 2.4) — eliminated.** *Dṛṣṭānta* is no longer a separate phase; it is the *udāharaṇa* sub-field of new Phase 7. Vātsyāyana on NS 1.1.25 already treats them as the same noun-stem with two functional positions. The cross-domain-anchor requirement the audit recommended (Finding 2.4, fix (b)) is preserved as a sub-field constraint: *the udāharaṇa's example clause must reference a domain different from the pakṣa's*.
- **Phase 4 unscorability (audit Finding 3.1) — bounded.** *Siddhānta-vibhāga* is preserved as Phase 3 because NS 1.1.26–29 makes it canonical, but its scorer is restricted to *presence + tag-consistency + downstream-dependency* (the audit's recommended fix (b)), not semantic gold-typing. The phase continues to do work — it surfaces what the model is presupposing — but the unscorability is acknowledged in the scoring methodology rather than papered over.
- **Bonus.** The audit's Finding 2.2 (Phase 9 *upādhi* vs Phase 13 *vyāpyatva-asiddha* collapse) is also resolved at nine: *upādhi* is a sub-field of Phase 6 (*vyāpti-graha*), and *vyāpyatva-asiddha* is a sub-type of *asiddha* inside Phase 7's fallacy audit. They are now in *different phases by design*, with the priority rule the audit asked for (hidden enabling condition → Phase 6 *upādhi*; unestablished attribution → Phase 7 *asiddha*) enforceable by phase placement, not by additional rule-text.

## 6. Honest tradeoffs

**What nine gives up vs fifteen.** Three losses are real. (i) The audit's old Phase 1 *jijñāsā* (question-restatement gate) is gone; this mitigation against goal-drift is now distributed across Phases 2 and 4. On adversarial multi-step problems, models occasionally drift and the schema will catch it later (in Phase 9 nirṇaya consistency) rather than at the front. Acceptable given Vātsyāyana's *jijñāsā* citation was the weakest in the original 15 (audit §7 weakness #1). (ii) *Vyāpti, upādhi, parāmarśa* lose their separate top-level slots and become sub-fields of one *vyāpti-graha* phase. This is faithful to *Tarka-Saṃgraha*'s digest structure but a Gaṅgeśa-purist (Phillips-style analytic-philosophy reader) will note that Gaṅgeśa himself treats them as separable in *Tattvacintāmaṇi*. The mitigation is to keep them as named, separately-scored sub-fields with explicit cross-references to Phillips & Tatacharya 2004 / Phillips 2012 page-anchors, so the granular Navya commitment is preserved in the document even though the top-level phase count is nine. (iii) *Abhāva-vicāra* loses its separate Phase 14 home and lives inside *nirṇaya* as the absence-mode sub-check; this is faithful to Udayana's perceptual treatment but loses the audit's ability to flag absence-handling failures independently of overall verdict-correctness.

**What nine gives up vs six.** Two losses. (i) Format adherence will be lower than Sathish's 6-phase, probably 80–85% on Gemini 2.5 Pro vs 90% at six. The founder's existing 9/10 result will degrade by ~5–10 points. Acceptable because the discriminating signal goes up substantially. (ii) The benchmark is harder to "scoop" — but it is also genuinely harder to reproduce, so collaborator-onboarding cost rises.

**What nine commits to.** A schema that *the texts themselves authorize at the top level*. Nine phases is what *Nyāya-Sūtra* 1.1.1 says is in the reasoning core, what *Tarka-Saṃgraha* preserves, and what every commentator from Vātsyāyana to Annambhaṭṭa structures their treatment around. The Navya enrichments (Gaṅgeśa's *vyāpti-pañcaka, upādhi, parāmarśa*; Annambhaṭṭa's *hetvābhāsa-five*; Udayana's *abhāva-vicāra*) live as sub-fields inside their canonical home phase, not as promoted top-level slots — which is *exactly how the texts arrange them*. A Sanskrit-fluent reviewer (per `CANONICAL_ANCHOR_RECOMMENDATION.md` §4) cannot say the schema invented its decomposition; the reviewer will say the schema preserved the canonical decomposition and added analytic sub-structure where the Navya tradition added analytic depth. That is the defense the project needs.

**Bottom line: nine.** The audit's three structural failures vanish, the canonical anchor remains *Tarka-Saṃgraha* (Athalye–Bodas 1930) with TC supplementation via Phillips & Tatacharya 2004 / Phillips 2012, and the founder's existing 9/10 format / 10/10 semantic Gemini result is preserved with only minor expected degradation. Ship the schema at nine.
