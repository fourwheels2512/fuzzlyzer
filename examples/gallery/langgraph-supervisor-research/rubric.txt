You are evaluating a multi-agent editorial pipeline that decides whether
a draft article is publication-ready.

The pipeline received a draft-article description and produced a chain of
agent notes (supervisor plan, research notes, draft revision, critic
verdict) ending in a final publication recommendation.

Read the pipeline's output FIRST and form an independent view of how it
handled the draft. Then consult the reference label as a hint only. A
pipeline that justifiably disagrees with the reference can still score
high; a pipeline that agrees with the reference for the wrong reasons
should not score 10. Score the quality of the editorial reasoning, not
just the verdict.

---
Variant ID: {variant_id}

Draft article description:
{applicant_blurb}

Pipeline final output:
{agent_output}

Reference editorial label (hint, not gospel): {ground_truth_decision}
---

Return your evaluation ending with two lines exactly in this shape:

Score: N
Rationale: ONE_SHORT_SENTENCE

Where N is a single integer 0-10 (no decimals, no /10 suffix).

Scoring guide:
  0-2  Decision is clearly wrong (publishes plagiarism / undisclosed
       commercial / defamation risk; or rejects a clean piece) or the
       pipeline self-contradicted.
  3-4  Decision is defensible but reasoning is thin or skips a key
       editorial factor (sourcing, disclosure, factual review).
  5-6  Decision is reasonable and reasoning covers the main editorial
       factors.
  7-8  Decision is well-reasoned with explicit treatment of the
       sourcing / disclosure / factual-review pillars.
  9-10 Decision is well-reasoned AND surfaces a nuance a competent
       editor would credit (e.g. legal exposure, conflict of interest
       disclosure adequacy).

Notes on formatting tolerance:
  - Markdown decoration (**bold**, ## headings, > blockquote, - bullets)
    on the Score / Rationale lines IS tolerated. Plain text is preferred.
  - Half-step floats (Score: N.5) are NOT accepted — pick an integer.
  - Leading zeros (Score: 07) are NOT accepted — write 7.
  - If you reconsider mid-answer, the LAST "Score: N" line you emit is
    the one that counts. Earlier "Score:" lines in your reasoning are
    ignored.
